\title{Pre-training Buys Ordering, Not Calibration:\\
An EveNet Data-Efficiency Test for \kl\ Density-Ratio Learning\\
in $HH\to\bbtt$ (CMS Full Simulation)}
\author{Working note --- \bbtt\ \kl\ NSBI project}
\date{15 July 2026 \\[2pt]
\small Gates G0 / G0.5 of \texttt{EVENET\_FEASIBILITY\_NOTE}; 90-run sweep,
branch \texttt{fm}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We test whether the pre-trained event-level foundation model (FM)
EveNet~\cite{Hsu:2026evenet} improves the data efficiency of \emph{density-ratio}
learning for a Higgs self-coupling (\kl) measurement via neural simulation-based
inference (NSBI), using CMS full-simulation $HH\to\bbtt$ events. Three arms ---
fine-tuned, frozen-backbone, and from-scratch --- identical in data, architecture and
head and differing \emph{only} in initialisation, were trained at six training
fractions $\times$ five seeds (90 runs) and evaluated on one fixed held-out split
against two axes fixed before unblinding: discrimination (weighted area under the
receiver-operating characteristic curve, AUC) and ratio calibration (integral and shape
closure). The result is a clean dissociation. On discrimination, pre-training wins
decisively and consistently: $+0.014$--$0.017$ AUC at every fraction $\ge 1\%$
(many standard deviations), equivalent to a $\mathbf{{\approx}3\text{--}4.5\times}$
data multiplier, with both pre-trained arms exceeding a feature-level gradient-boosted
decision tree (BDT) ceiling. On calibration, pre-training buys \emph{nothing}: no arm
passes the closure gates at any fraction, the pre-registered adoption metric (closure
left-shift) is undefined, and the arms trade places within seed errors. Seed-ensembling
does not change this, because the closure offsets are same-sign systematic
($\Eref[\hat r]-1 \approx +0.13$--$0.29$) rather than zero-centred. The
frozen arm matches fine-tuning everywhere, locating the value in the pre-trained
\emph{representation} rather than in backbone adaptation. We argue the calibration
failure is a property of EveNet's noise-conditioned classification objective rather
than of the task, note that the feature BDT reaches $|\IC| = 0.018$ where EveNet
reaches $0.13$--$0.17$ \emph{despite worse AUC}, and propose a single decisive ablation.
\end{abstract}

\section{What was tested}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Physics task.} Learn per-event density ratios $r(x;\kl,\kl')$ between
\kl\ hypotheses for use in an unbinned NSBI likelihood. Discrimination between \kl\
points is driven by the \mhh\ lineshape and triangle--box interference, i.e.\ by a
\emph{continuous} deformation of one process's spectrum rather than by separation of
two distinct processes.

\paragraph{Data.} CMS 2024 full-simulation $HH\to\bbtt$ Powheg samples, passed through
the KIT CROWN analysis chain (published $\mu\tau_h$/$e\tau_h$ selection), converted to
EveNet's stock 7-feature token schema: four tokens per event ($b_1$, $b_2$, \tauh,
$\ell$), missing transverse energy in the global conditioning block, weights
$=$ \texttt{genWeight} $\times$ \texttt{puweight}. Requiring a valid $H\to b\bar b$
candidate drops 18.6--24.5\% of events (\kl-dependent: softer \kl$=5$ spectrum
$\Rightarrow$ more failed second-$b$ reconstruction). Class 0 $=$ \kl$=1$ (SM
reference, 488k events), class 1 $=$ \kl$=5$ (339k). The merged, shuffled sample is
split $80/10/10$; \textbf{all} runs share one fixed 82.7k-event test split.

\paragraph{The contrast.} Three arms differing only in initialisation:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,style=nextline]
\item[finetune] 20M-parameter pre-trained checkpoint, all weights trainable.
\item[frozen] same checkpoint, backbone (GlobalEmbedding, PET, ObjectEncoder) frozen,
  heads trainable --- isolates the \emph{representation} from optimiser effects.
\item[scratch] random initialisation --- the no-FM control.
\end{description}
Each at training fractions $\{0.003, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 1.0\}$
($\approx 2.5$k to 830k events) $\times$ 5 seeds $=$ 90 runs. Options files are the
authors' own downstream recipes (fine-tune and from-scratch variants of the
Exotic-Higgs study), vendored unchanged apart from the freeze flags.

\paragraph{External bar.} A gradient-boosted decision tree on the 10 high-level
features behind the published unbinned result, measured at full statistics on the same
physics (Sec.~\ref{sec:ceiling}).

\section{Pre-registration record}
\label{sec:prereg}

The following were fixed \emph{before} any sweep output existed, and are reproduced
here unchanged:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(P\arabic*),leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \textbf{Decision primacy (2026-07-12).} The adoption metric is the \emph{closure
  left-shift}: the smallest training fraction at which an arm's seed-mean passes both
  gates, compared between finetune and scratch; their ratio is the equivalent-data
  multiplier. A left-shift is an adoption-grade win \emph{even at AUC parity}; an
  AUC-only win with failing closure is \emph{not} a win. AUC serves as a secondary
  no-regression guard.
\item \textbf{Gates.} $|\IC| \le \max(1\%,\,3\times\text{stat})$ \emph{and}
  stat-debiased shape RMS $\le 0.05$ ($=$ the analysis \texttt{CLOSURE\_TOL}), with
  $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}\approx 1$ indicating noise-only residuals.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint selection (2026-07-14).} Every arm is evaluated at its
  best-validation-loss checkpoint (EveNet's predictor otherwise loads the final, i.e.\
  most overfit, epoch).
\item \textbf{Fallback.} Should no arm pass the gates anywhere --- leaving the
  left-shift undefined --- the secondary reading is relative closure at matched
  fractions, reported as such.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The fallback (P4) is the branch that actually materialised.

\section{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\subsection{The closure identity}

For scores $p$, labels $y$ and weights $w$ on the test split, the ratio estimate is
$\hat r = \frac{p}{1-p}\cdot f_{\text{prior}}$, with $f_{\text{prior}}$ the inverse of
the class prior the training loss effectively used
(measured, Sec.~\ref{sec:corrections}). Everything below rests on one identity. Average
the \emph{true} ratio over events drawn from the reference:
%
\begin{equation}
\Eref[r] \;=\; \int p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)\,
\frac{p_{\mathrm{hyp}}(x)}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
\;=\; \int p_{\mathrm{hyp}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x \;=\; 1 .
\label{eq:closure}
\end{equation}
%
The reference density cancels against the denominator and what remains is a normalised
probability density. So the true ratio, averaged over reference events, is \emph{exactly}
one --- for any pair of hypotheses, with no free parameters. Intuitively, $r(x)$ says
``this event is $r$ times more likely under the hypothesis''; over the reference ensemble
the regions where $r>1$ and $r<1$ must balance precisely. On a weighted Monte Carlo
sample the expectation becomes the self-normalised weighted average
$\sum_{i\in\mathrm{ref}} w_i \hat r_i / \sum_{i\in\mathrm{ref}} w_i$, which converges to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:closure} at rate $1/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ --- hence every closure number
carries a statistical error and is ``consistent with 1'', never ``equal to 1''.

\subsection{The two metrics}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,style=nextline]
\item[{Integral closure, $\IC = \Eref[\hat r]-1$}]
  \emph{Definition:} the weighted mean of $\hat r$ over held-out reference events, minus
  one; quoted with its weighted standard error.
  \emph{Intuition:} reweight the reference sample by $\hat r$ and ask whether you recover
  the \emph{total} hypothesis yield. $\IC = +0.20$ means the reweighted reference
  over-produces the hypothesis by 20\%. It is the \emph{normalisation} or ``exchange
  rate'' error of the ratio. Two-bin illustration: reference $(0.5, 0.5)$, hypothesis
  $(0.8, 0.2)$ gives true $r = (1.6, 0.4)$ and $\Eref[r] = 0.5(1.6)+0.5(0.4) = 1$
  exactly; a miscalibrated $\hat r = (2.0, 0.4)$ gives $1.2$, i.e.\ $\IC = +20\%$.
\item[{Shape closure, $\mathrm{SC_{rms}}$ and \SCn}]
  \emph{Definition:} quantile-bin the score; in each bin compare the $\hat r$-reweighted
  reference mass $A_b$ with the actual hypothesis mass $B_b$, forming
  $\mathrm{rel}_b = A_b/B_b - 1$; report the RMS over bins with each bin's statistical
  variance ($1/n_{\mathrm{eff},0} + 1/n_{\mathrm{eff},1}$) subtracted in quadrature, so
  finite-sample noise does not masquerade as bias.
  \emph{Intuition:} $\IC$ can be satisfied by errors that cancel globally --- too large
  here, too small there. The shape test therefore applies Eq.~\eqref{eq:closure}
  \emph{differentially}: after reweighting the reference by $\hat r$, it must become
  statistically indistinguishable from the hypothesis sample \emph{bin by bin}, which is
  precisely what ``being the density ratio'' means. We bin in the classifier score
  because the score is monotone in $\hat r$ itself, so any miscalibration is forced to
  show up there.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why the per-bin error is $1/n_{\mathrm{eff}}$.} Both $A_b$ and $B_b$ are
Monte Carlo sums over finite, \emph{weighted} samples, so both fluctuate. For a weighted
sum $S = \sum_i v_i$ over events whose presence is independent (Poisson), the variance is
$\mathrm{Var}(S) = \sum_i v_i^2$, and therefore the \emph{relative} variance is
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{Var}(S)}{S^2} \;=\; \frac{\sum_i v_i^2}{\big(\sum_i v_i\big)^2}
\;\equiv\; \frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}},
\qquad
n_{\mathrm{eff}} \;\equiv\; \frac{\big(\sum_i v_i\big)^2}{\sum_i v_i^2},
\label{eq:neff}
\end{equation}
%
which is precisely the definition of the effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Kish).
For unweighted events ($v_i = 1$) it collapses to $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = N$ and to the
familiar Poisson result $\sqrt{N}/N = 1/\sqrt{N}$; unequal weights always reduce it.
Concretely, $v = (1,1,1,1)$ gives $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = 16/4 = 4$, whereas
$v = (10,1,1,1)$ gives $169/103 = 1.6$: four events that behave statistically like $1.6$,
because a single event carries most of the sum. In our bins $v_i = w_i \hat r_i$ on the
reference side --- the reweighting inflates the weight spread, which is why bins at
$\hat r \to \infty$ are dropped by the $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ threshold rather than allowed to
dominate --- and $v_i = w_i$ on the hypothesis side. Since $\mathrm{rel}_b = A_b/B_b - 1$
is a ratio of estimates from two \emph{independent} samples, the relative variances add:
$\sigma_b^2 = 1/n_{\mathrm{eff},0} + 1/n_{\mathrm{eff},1}$.

\paragraph{Why the subtraction is in quadrature.} Write
$\mathrm{rel}_b = \beta_b + \varepsilon_b$, with $\beta_b$ the genuine bias and
$\varepsilon_b$ zero-mean noise of variance $\sigma_b^2$. Then
$\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{rel}_b^2] = \beta_b^2 + \sigma_b^2$ --- the naive RMS is inflated by
noise even for a perfectly calibrated model, and increasingly so at small statistics,
exactly where the interesting comparisons live. Averaging over bins and subtracting the
known $\sigma_b^2$ therefore estimates the bias RMS,
$\mathrm{SC_{rms}} = \sqrt{\max\big(\langle \mathrm{rel}^2 - \sigma^2 \rangle,\, 0\big)}$.
The clipping at zero is why exact $0.000$ entries appear in Table~\ref{tab:closure}: they
mean ``no bias resolvable above the statistical floor'', not literal perfection. Without
the subtraction the metric rejects correct models --- the first implementation did
precisely that, failing an analytically calibrated toy in three seeds out of four. The
same $\sigma_b$ normalises the reported
$\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf} = \langle (\mathrm{rel}_b/\sigma_b)^2 \rangle$, which is $\approx 1$
when the residuals are pure noise and $\gg 1$ when they are not.

\subsection{$\mathrm{SC_{rms}}$ versus \SCn}

\paragraph{Mechanically.} $\mathrm{SC_{rms}}$ is computed from $\hat r$ as delivered;
\SCn\ from $\hat r/\Eref[\hat r]$, i.e.\ after forcing the integral closure to zero. The
difference is exactly the contamination of the shape metric by the normalisation error.
Suppose $\hat r = c\,r_{\mathrm{true}}$ --- a \emph{pure} scale error with a perfect
shape. Then in every bin $A_b/B_b = c$, so $\mathrm{rel}_b = c-1$ for all $b$ and
$\mathrm{SC_{rms}} \simeq |c-1| \simeq |\IC|$, even though nothing about the shape is
wrong. Dividing by $\Eref[\hat r] = c$ first gives $\SCn = 0$. The two raw numbers are
therefore \emph{not} independent, and $\mathrm{SC_{rms}}$ alone cannot distinguish ``the
ratio is the wrong size'' from ``the ratio is the wrong function''. Reading them together
gives three clean signatures:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccl@{}}
\toprule
signature & $|\IC|$ & $\mathrm{SC_{rms}}$ & \SCn & diagnosis \\
\midrule
calibrated & $\approx 0$ & $\approx 0$ & $\approx 0$ & usable \\
pure scale error & $|c-1|$ & $\approx |\IC|$ & $\approx 0$ & normalisation only --- removable \\
shape distortion & any & large & large & deformed likelihood --- not removable \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{How to read the pair. Validated on synthetic data: a $\times1.4$ scale error
gives $(0.399,\,0.399,\,0.000)$; a temperature-$1.5$ distortion gives
$(0.450,\,0.694,\,0.503)$.}
\label{tab:signatures}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Physically --- why the distinction is not cosmetic.} In an NSBI likelihood the
contribution of process $p$ enters as $\nu_p\,p_p(x)$, with $\nu_p$ the expected yield
(from cross section $\times$ luminosity $\times$ acceptance, determined independently of
the classifier) and $p_p(x) = r_p(x)\,p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)$. If $\hat r_p = c\,r_p$, then
%
\begin{equation}
\nu_p\,\hat p_p(x) \;=\; \big(c\,\nu_p\big)\,\Big[\hat p_p(x)/c\Big],
\end{equation}
%
i.e.\ the constant is algebraically indistinguishable from having mis-stated the
\emph{yield} by the factor $c$ while leaving the normalised per-event shape untouched.
Two consequences follow. First, it is \emph{removable by construction}: rescaling
$\hat r_p \to \hat r_p/\Eref[\hat r_p]$ on held-out reference events restores
$\Eref = 1$ exactly, at the cost only of the statistical error on that single number.
Second, uncorrected, it propagates into the fit \emph{solely} through that process's
normalisation, where it is degenerate with a rate nuisance parameter. For a coupling
measurement this is emphatically not harmless --- $\sigma(\kl)$ is exactly what carries
the physics, so an uncorrected $c$ biases \kl\ --- but it is a one-parameter defect with
a one-parameter cure, and it does not touch the discriminant.

A shape error is different in kind. There $\hat r(x)/r(x)$ varies with $x$, so events are
mis-weighted \emph{relative to one another}: the per-event likelihood is deformed, no
single constant repairs it, and no rate parameter absorbs it, so the induced bias on \kl\
survives profiling. This is why we gate on both, and why \SCn\ --- reported alongside,
never instead of, the pre-registered pair --- is the number that says whether a failure
is repairable.

\paragraph{Which signature our data shows.} At full statistics all three arms sit close
to the ``pure scale'' row of Table~\ref{tab:signatures}: frozen
$(|\IC|,\mathrm{SC_{rms}},\SCn) = (0.171,\,0.163,\,0.044)$, finetune
$(0.133,\,0.115,\,0.074)$, scratch $(0.156,\,0.194,\,0.084)$. The residual is
predominantly a normalisation error with a comparatively small shape component --- which
is precisely the fingerprint of a mis-specified prior factor in the ratio conversion, and
the reason the outstanding check of Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism} targets exactly that.

\noindent \SCn\ is a \emph{post-hoc} diagnostic added during analysis and is reported
alongside, never instead of, the pre-registered gates. Validation: on
synthetic data a pure $\times1.4$ scale error gives $\mathrm{SC_{rms}} = 0.399$ but
$\SCn = 0.000$, while a genuine temperature distortion gives $0.694$ and $0.503$
respectively.

\paragraph{What closure cannot catch.} Both metrics test \emph{self-consistency}, not
\emph{sufficiency}. A classifier trained on lossy features $\phi(x)$ converges to the
exact ratio of the \emph{marginalised} densities
$p_{\mathrm{hyp}}(\phi)/p_{\mathrm{ref}}(\phi)$, which satisfies
Eq.~\eqref{eq:closure} identically and passes any test binned in a function of $\phi$
--- the degenerate estimator $\hat r \equiv 1$ passes both metrics perfectly while
carrying no information at all. Information loss does not appear as bias; it appears as
a \emph{flatter} ratio, i.e.\ as lost discrimination. Closure therefore catches lies, not
omissions: it earns $\hat r$ the right to enter the likelihood, while the AUC axis
(Sec.~\ref{sec:auc}) is what shows the ratio is worth entering. This is also why a
score-binned closure test cannot substitute for the analysis-level closure in physical
observables, where the model is asked to reproduce distributions it was not free to
choose the coordinates of.

global batch of $\approx 8$k across 4 GPUs, one ``epoch'' is a single gradient step, and
the selected checkpoints sit at epochs 4--21, i.e.\ the models are compared after
$\mathcal{O}(10)$ updates under \emph{different} learning-rate recipes (the fine-tune
recipe uses an aggressive head learning rate over a small body one; the scratch recipe
is uniform). We regard this tier as a recipe artefact pending a batch-size/learning-rate
ablation, not as evidence about representations.

\paragraph{Verdict under (P1)/(P2): a null.} The first-passing fraction is
\texttt{none} for all three arms, so the closure left-shift --- the pre-registered
adoption metric --- is undefined. Under the (P4) fallback, the arms overlap within
seed errors and \emph{trade places}: scratch is nominally best at $0.01$--$0.05$
(largest gap at $0.02$, ${\approx}2.3\sigma$), the pre-trained arms at $0.1$ and $1.0$.
There is no consistent ordering and no left-shift.

\paragraph{The failure is statistics-limited, not fixed.} \SCn\ falls monotonically
with training fraction ($0.32\to0.07$ finetune, $0.47\to0.04$ frozen, $0.35\to0.08$
scratch) and $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ drops from $60$--$190$ to $6$--$15$. The estimator
does converge; it simply has not reached the 1\%/5\% gates by 830k events in any arm.

\paragraph{Ensembling does not rescue it.} Because $\Eref$ is linear in $r$, the
ensemble ratio $\bar r = \frac1K\sum_k r_k$ satisfies the identity
$\IC_{\text{ens}} = \frac1K\sum_k \IC_k$ exactly: the ensemble carries no information
beyond the \emph{signed} mean of the members' offsets. The measurement is unambiguous
--- in 15 of 18 cells the per-seed mean $|\IC|$ and the signed mean are numerically
identical (e.g.\ finetune $0.02$: $0.2917$ vs.\ $0.2917$; frozen $1.0$: $0.1706$ vs.\
$0.1706$), i.e.\ \textbf{all five seeds miss in the same direction}. The offsets are a
same-sign systematic ($\Eref[\hat r] - 1 \approx +0.13$ to $+0.29$), not zero-centred
model noise, so there is nothing for averaging to cancel. (Ensembling does help in the
three mixed-sign cells: finetune $0.003$, $0.130\to0.038$; scratch $0.01$,
$0.237\to0.047$; scratch $0.05$, $0.124\to0.043$.) Ensemble AUC improves slightly for
all arms and preserves the FM advantage (Table~\ref{tab:auc}).

\noindent Two comparisons matter, and they point in opposite directions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Discrimination: EveNet wins.} At $0.1$ and $1.0$ both pre-trained arms

  object-level model extracts \kl\ information the 10 aggregate features discard.
  \emph{Caveat:} the ceiling was measured on the un-cut flat-feature population,
  whereas the sweep applies the valid-$b$-pair requirement, so the sweep population is
  slightly easier; the margin at $1.0$ ($+0.017$) is larger than we would expect this
  effect to explain, but the comparison carries an asterisk that the arm-to-arm
  comparisons do not.
\item \textbf{Calibration: the BDT wins by an order of magnitude.} At full statistics
  the ceiling reaches $|\IC| = 0.018$ and shape $0.000$; every EveNet arm sits at
  $|\IC| = 0.13$--$0.17$. \emph{A simple boosted-tree ensemble with worse AUC produces
  a far better-calibrated ratio.} This is the observation that shifts the
  interpretation from ``this task is hard to calibrate'' to ``this training objective
  does not calibrate'' (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Interpretation: a dissociation, and a candidate mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\paragraph{The headline.} Pre-training improved \emph{ordering} substantially and
\emph{calibration} not at all. These are separable properties: AUC is invariant under
any monotone remap of the score, and an entire family of classifiers shares a given
AUC while only one member is the density ratio. A method evaluated solely by
significance-improvement characteristic (SIC) or background rejection --- as in the
EveNet paper~\cite{Hsu:2026evenet} and the controlled objective
study~\cite{Elsharkawy:2026} respectively --- would report an unambiguous success here
and would be structurally unable to see the calibration null. For NSBI, where the
per-event ratio enters the likelihood, the calibration axis is the binding one.

\paragraph{Candidate mechanism (testable).} EveNet's classification head is trained
under diffusion-time conditioning: the loss is $\alpha^2(t)\,\mathrm{CE}(y,\hat y)$
with a noising probability that our configuration --- following the authors' own
downstream recipe --- sets to $1.0$, while prediction is performed at the clean
endpoint. A $t$-averaged cross-entropy is \emph{not} a proper scoring rule for the
$t=0$ posterior: its minimiser preserves the ranking of events (hence the strong AUC)
while the logit \emph{scale} need not equal that of the clean-input posterior (hence
the systematic $\Eref[\hat r] > 1$). Three observations are consistent with this and
inconsistent with a residual convention error: the offsets are same-sign across seeds
and arms \emph{including scratch}; they vary cell-to-cell rather than being a common
constant (a wrong prior factor would give an identical $\IC$ everywhere); and they
correlate cell-by-cell with \SCn\ (frozen $0.003$: $0.47$/$0.31$; frozen $1.0$:
$0.17$/$0.00$), as expected if both are driven by per-event log-ratio noise --- for
which $\Eref[\hat r] \approx e^{\sigma^2/2} > 1$, with $\Eref[\hat r] = 1.2$
corresponding to $\sigma \approx 0.6$.

\paragraph{The decisive ablation.} Re-run one column of the grid (say fraction $1.0$,
all three arms, 5 seeds $=$ 15 runs, hours) with the classification noising disabled
(\texttt{noise\_prob: [0.0, 0.0]}), everything else identical. If $|\IC|$ collapses
toward the ceiling's $0.018$, the calibration null is a fixable property of the
training recipe and the FM programme continues on a corrected objective. If it does
not, the null is a property of the pre-trained representation's transfer to continuous
ratio estimation, and the objective-mismatch verdict of the feasibility note stands.

\section{Corrections applied during analysis}
\label{sec:corrections}

For reproducibility, three defects were found and fixed \emph{after} predictions
existed but \emph{before} the numbers above were read. All are estimator/plumbing
fixes, not changes to the decision rule.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Prior convention (largest effect).} The evaluator initially applied
  $f_{\text{prior}} = W_0/W_1 = 0.556$ (raw-weight training). Scanning the three
  candidate conventions on all 15 full-statistics runs selected \emph{class-balanced}
  ($f = 1$) unanimously; EveNet equalises classes internally. The spurious factor had
  produced $|\IC| \approx 0.30$--$0.47$ \emph{flat in} $N$ and inflated shape closure
  by the same constant (a scale error shifts every bin equally). This is why the
  corrected numbers, unlike the first pass, decrease with statistics.
\item \textbf{Weight treatment.} Closure now uses $|w|$: binary cross-entropy training
  cannot consume negative weights, so the reference density the model saw is the $|w|$
  one (the reference sample carries 6.1\% negative weights).
\item \textbf{Predict-path configuration.} Predict configs inherited a relative
  \texttt{options.yaml} path that resolved into the (empty) config farm, and lacked the
  head-inclusion block, so every prediction task died before loading a checkpoint. Both
  are now generated from the matching training config.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Caveats and scope}
\label{sec:caveats}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{No sensitivity claim.} No $\sigma_{\kl}$ or absolute-sensitivity
  statement is made or implied; the stock schema discards $\tau$-identification and
  FastMTT $m_{\tau\tau}$ (equally in all arms), and the MC-statistics estimator
  question remains open upstream~\cite{Shyamsundar:2025}.
\item \textbf{One channel, one hypothesis pair.} \kl$=1$ vs.\ \kl$=5$, $\mu\tau_h$ and
  $e\tau_h$ only. The \kl$=0$ pair and the systematic-variation task (Money Plot 2,
  nominal vs.\ injected $\mathrm{JES}(\mhh)$) are untouched.
\item \textbf{Population mismatch vs.\ the ceiling only} (Sec.~\ref{sec:ceiling}); all
  arm-to-arm comparisons share identical events.
\item \textbf{Five seeds} give seed errors of $0.03$--$0.17$ on \SCn, which is why the
  closure comparison is a null rather than a measured equality.
\item \textbf{The 0.003 tier} is optimiser-transient-dominated (Sec.~\ref{sec:auc}).
\item One frozen cell ($0.02$, seed 3) and one scratch run ($0.01$, seed 2 ---
  early-stopped at epoch 1) are degraded; both inflate their cells' seed spread and
  neither changes a conclusion.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implications and next steps}
\label{sec:next}

\paragraph{For the FM programme.} G0 passes on discrimination with a $3$--$4.5\times$
data multiplier; G0.5 is a null. Under the pre-registered rule (P1) that combination is
\emph{not} adoption for NSBI --- but it is also not the objective-mismatch verdict the
feasibility note feared, because the representation demonstrably carries \kl-relevant
information (frozen $\approx$ finetune, both above the ceiling on AUC). The programme's
decision therefore hinges on the ablation of Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism}, which is cheap.

\paragraph{Priorities.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{N\arabic*}.,leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \textbf{[P0, S]} Noising ablation at fraction $1.0$ (15 runs) --- decides whether
  the calibration null is recipe or representation.
\item \textbf{[P1, S]} G1 corner-encoding A/B (\tauh\ at the vacant flag corner): the
  NPZs already exist for both encodings.
\item \textbf{[P1, M]} Money Plot 2 (systematic-variation data efficiency): the FM's
  low-statistics claim aimed at nuisance parameterisation, using the analytic injector
  where closure is exact by construction.
\item \textbf{[P2, S]} Low-fraction recipe ablation (batch size / learning rate at
  0.003) to convert the inversion from artefact to measurement.
\item \textbf{[P2, S]} Re-measure the ceiling on the valid-$b$-pair population to
  remove the asterisk in Sec.~\ref{sec:ceiling}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{For the EveNet authors (discussion, 16 July 2026).} Three concrete items:
(i) is a calibration-preserving classification mode available or planned --- i.e.\ a
$t=0$-only classification loss --- given that the ranking transfers but the calibrated
ratio does not? (ii) our G1 result will be the first data on reusing \emph{vacant flag
corners} for object types absent from pre-training (\tauh, $\gamma$); would they bless
this as the zero-surgery interim, and (iii) would they consider retrofitting an
explicit type-embedding table --- initialised from the current flag patterns so it is
function-preserving on day one --- so that future object types become an
append-a-row operation rather than schema surgery?
