Data-driven background mechanics —
and the bridge to your NSBI fit

The mechanics companion to Lesson 6: how each data-driven method actually constructs its estimate, why none of them hands you a per-event density, and the one trick (classifier-to-reference) that turns any of them into the ratio your NSBI likelihood consumes.

Three methods, three different objects out — and one universal adapter. Keep the spine in view: ABCD → a histogram; fake-factor → a weighted sample; embedding → a sample. NSBI needs r(x)=p(x)/pref(x). The bridge (§5) is how you get there from any of them.

1 · ABCD (QCD multijet) — a counting construction

Two variables assumed independent for the background (e.g. di-τ charge OS/SS × τ isolation tight/anti) carve four orthogonal regions; D (OS+tight) is signal-like, so signal contaminates only D and the three sidebands stay ~pure QCD.

2 · Fake-factor (jet→τh) — a reweighting construction

Measure a tight-to-loose ratio for fakes in a fake-enriched determination region (after subtracting genuine taus), then apply it as a per-event weight to a loose-ID control sample with signal-region kinematics.

3 · Embedding (Z→ττ) — a hybrid construction

The most unlike the other two: take a real Z→μμ data event, surgically remove the two muons, and replace them with simulated τ leptons carrying the muons' momenta — which then decay. The pileup, jets, underlying event and detector conditions are real data; only the τ decay and its detector response are simulated.cites: arXiv:1903.01216

Each gives a yield, a histogram, or a (weighted) sample — never a function you can evaluate at an arbitrary point x. That single fact is the whole reason the next step exists.

5 · The bridge — classifier-to-reference (CARL)

NSBI needs rc(x)=pc(x)/pref(x) for every component. You get it without writing down any density: train a binary classifier to separate a sample of the component from a sample of the reference. With balanced classes and a proper loss, the optimal score s(x)=pc/(pc+pref), so rc(x)=s(x)/(1−s(x)).cites: arXiv:1506.02169 (CARL)

Why this homogenizes the fit: once each data-driven background is a ratio to the same Pref, the likelihood doesn't care that QCD came from ABCDnn and tt̄ came from MC — they enter the mixture in the same currency. Your preliminary result already runs this machinery for the MC components (calibrated per-event ratio classifiers, isotonic calibration, label-flip for negative weights, a single distant κλ=5 reference).
The honest framing. The classifier-to-reference step is the easy, solved middle. The work is on the two ends: upstream, getting a faithful high-dimensional sample of the data-driven background (the ABCDnn-in-10-D problem); and downstream, propagating systematics as a per-event morphing of r(x) (the learned-morphing burden from Lesson 4). The bridge isn't the bottleneck — it's the well-lit middle of a path whose two ends are where the real risk lives.
