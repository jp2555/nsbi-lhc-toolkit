The ATLAS off-shell NSBI recipe —
and what changes for κλ

The first full NSBI measurement (ATLAS off-shell H→ZZ→4ℓ, HIGG-2023-01 / Sandesara et al. / 2412.01600) is the closest template for your di-Higgs κλ analysis. Here's its systematics recipe (Sec 5), the mapping to κλ, and where di-Higgs gets harder.

Design goal of their Sec 5: get nuisance-parameter dependence into a per-event likelihood without training one giant network on ~100 NPs. The trick is factorization.

1 · The recipe (Sec 5)

  1. Reference hypothesis. Write everything relative to a fixed Pref(x)=P(x|μ=1,α=0); every NN estimates a ratio to that reference. NPs enter both the Poisson rate ν(μ,α) and the unbinned density, each constrained by a Gaussian term (5.1.1).
  2. Factorize the systematic dependence. Split the per-event density ratio into an α-independent piece (trained once) × a systematic weight that factorizes over NPs:
    PX(x,α) = wX(x,α) · [nominal ratio],   wX(x,α) = ∏m wX(x,αm) each NP an independent source; each single-NP weight built by the standard HistFactory polynomial-exponential interpolation — but applied per event (5.1.2–5.1.3)
    They explicitly reject feeding α into the NN (Cranmer-style) — un-trainable for O(100) NPs.
  3. Learn the ±1σ per-event weights. Each wX(x,α±) = PX(x|α±)/PX(x|0) is itself a density ratio (varied vs nominal sample) → a separate classifier, trained with the same cross-entropy trick: w = ŝ/(1−ŝ). Two NNs per (NP × process) → ~O(2000) networks here (5.1.4).
  4. MC-stat uncertainty as its own NP. The NN-ensemble spread is the training-stat uncertainty (σ = σens/√Nens); propagate it as a single coherent nuisance αstat, calibrated against the spurious spread of μ̂ (5.2).
  5. The fit. Custom JAX + iminuit (RooFit/HistFactory is binned-only): MIGRAD profile fits, exact gradients/Hessian, and an interference-aware impact definition (implicit-function theorem, not finite-difference) because interference makes the standard impact ambiguous (5.3).

In one line: train the α=0 ratios once, train ~2000 ±1σ per-event systematic-weight ratios, interpolate poly-exp per NP, multiply, profile in JAX. This is the R2 approach from Lesson 3 — factorized, fixed-order, two-classifiers-per-NP.

Pieces worth stealing wholesale for κλ: the reference-hypothesis trick; the MC-stat-as-an-NP (you're stat-starved, so you need it); and especially the interference-aware impact definition — κλ has the same interference-impact ambiguity, so you'll need exactly this.

2 · Mapping it to κλ (yes, it's a baseline)

Off-shell is the only published "a coupling morphs an interference shape" NSBI — structurally identical to κλ. The one substitution: their POI coefficients become the κλ morphing basis.

off-shell:   (μ−√μ)·S + √μ·SBI + (1−√μ)·B   ⟶   κλ:   κλ²·|triangle|² + κλ·interference + 1·|box|² same search-oriented mixture model (Eq 5.6); replace the √μ interference structure with the quadratic {κλ², κλ, 1} from gg→HH = κλ·triangle + box

Everything else — reference trick, factorized systematic weights, MC-stat NP, JAX fit — carries over directly. Use it as your skeleton.

The four bb̄ττ-specific hardships, in plain terms: (i) the κλ–systematic shape degeneracy is acute because JES/τES move mHH (4ℓ mostly dodges this — leptons are clean); (ii) many more, larger experimental systematics; (iii) data-driven fakes have no natural per-event density; (iv) far lower data signal yield (intrinsic, fb-scale σ) — though ample MC (≈500k–750k/λ) keeps the morphing well-trained, so this actually favours NSBI (per-event info matters most when events are few); plus (v) κλ-dependent theory entangles the systematic with the POI.

The implication. Adopt the off-shell recipe as your foundation — especially the reference trick, MC-stat-as-NP, and the interference-aware JAX impact. But its factorized, fixed-order, ~2000-NN morphing — fine for clean 4ℓ — is exactly the part the harder bb̄ττ systematics stress. That's why the plan is R2 as skeleton, with the morphing upgraded by R4 (expressive, non-factorizable, fewer estimators) and R3 (honest, since you're stat-starved). Neat footnote: J. Sandesara (lead author of this baseline) is also on the HistFactory-v2/GP work (R3) — the same group is already building the upgrade.

4 · How the current CMS bb̄ττ analysis does systematics — and how NSBI compares

The CMS Run-II HH→bb̄ττ analysis (AN-18-121, Sec 9) is a binned-template / Combine fit. Systematics split into normalisation NPs (lumi, e/μ-ID, prefiring, PU, tt̄/QCD/DY norms, background cross-sections, VBF dipole-recoil, and a κλ-dependent theory NP THU_HH = +4/−18% at SM) and shape NPs. The shape mechanism (their words): "for each source the relevant objects are shifted up and down with all depending objects (shifting τ also shifts MET and SVfit) and the final discriminants are recomputed" — i.e. shift objects → re-run the (fixed, trained) DNN/multiclass → re-histogram → up/down templates. The observable is the binned DNN score (Bayesian-optimised binning); κλ enters by reweighting the signal MC (the 2D mHH×cosθ* / NLO reweighting).

The key point — and a correction to a strawman. CMS does not bin mHH; it bins a multivariate DNN score, propagating systematics by re-running that DNN on varied inputs. So CMS already carries the same multi-dimensional systematic information NSBI does — NSBI is not "exposed to more systematics" than the real CMS fit.

So the real differences are just two (not "exposure"): (1) counted vs learned — CMS histograms its re-run DNN (no new estimation); NSBI must train the per-event systematic weight, adding the one ML layer that can be wrong even when the physics is fine (→ what validation / calibration / C2ST guard); (2) what NSBI buys — unbinning + a κλ-optimal ratio (CMS's DNN is a single fixed discriminant). The off-shell ~2× and FAIR-Universe ~20% gains are exactly these. Everything hard and shared (κλ theory, JES×τES factorization, QCD/fake modelling) is the same either way.

Reframed plan. NSBI for κλ is best seen as "replace CMS's binned-DNN observable with an unbinned, κλ-optimal per-event likelihood, keeping the identical systematic NPs." The gain is information (unbinning + optimality); the new cost is the learned morphing + coverage validation; the systematics budget carries over unchanged. The danger of "worse than CMS" is the learned layer being mis-calibrated / under-validated — not a heavier systematics burden.

Check yourself

1. What is the single idea that lets the off-shell analysis avoid a 100-NP network?

Factorization: split the per-event ratio into an α-independent piece (trained once) × a product of single-NP systematic weights, each built by per-event interpolation between separately-trained ±1σ ratio classifiers.

2. Which off-shell ingredients should you copy directly for κλ?

The reference-hypothesis trick, MC-statistical-uncertainty-as-an-NP, and the interference-aware impact definition (κλ has the same interference ambiguity). And the mixture-model structure, with {μ−√μ,√μ,1−√μ} → {κλ²,κλ,1}.

3. Why is the off-shell morphing "good enough" there but stressed for bb̄ττ?

4ℓ is clean — its big systematics (theory) are separable from the signal shape, so a fixed-order factorized morphing suffices. bb̄ττ's big systematics (JES/τES) deform mHH itself (degenerate with κλ), there are far more of them, and signal stats are tiny — so the morphing quality becomes the limiting factor → R4/R3.
