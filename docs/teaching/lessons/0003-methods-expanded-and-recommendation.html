The five methods, expanded —
and what fits HH→bb̄ττ / κλ

Builds on Lesson 2 (the vocabulary) and Lesson 1 (the map). Reminder of the one idea: every method is a way to build the morphing Δ(x|α) — how each event's probability bends as you turn the systematic knobs — or, for RS3L, a way to avoid needing it.

1 · The methods, in depth

R1 · Ghosh–Nachman–Whiteson · PRD 104 056026 (2021) · 2105.08742

Uncertainty-aware learning — the principle everything rests on

IdeaThe old instinct was to make a classifier blind to systematics ("decorrelation" / "pivoting" / adversarial training) so the result is robust. This paper shows the opposite is better: make the classifier aware of the nuisance and let the fit handle the dependence.
MechanismFeed the nuisance knob α to the network as an input. The per-event score then depends on α, and the profile likelihood uses that dependence rather than discarding it.
StrengthBetter sensitivity than forced invariance — you keep information instead of throwing it away. This is the justification for all of R2–R4.
LimitA principle + toy demonstration (Gaussian, H→ττ), not a full recipe. It doesn't tell you how to build Δ(x|α) efficiently for hundreds of knobs.
R2 · ATLAS · Rep. Prog. Phys. 88 (2025) · 2412.01600

ATLAS NSBI — the first full-scale, real measurement

IdeaCarry per-event NSBI all the way through a real analysis (off-shell H→ZZ→4ℓ) with a large set of systematics — and produce proper confidence intervals.
MechanismFor each nuisance, train classifiers that learn the per-event ratio between the "knob = ±1" sample and the nominal sample, then interpolate to build Δ(x|α). Wrap it in a profile-likelihood fit (test statistic tμ), with explicit diagnostics and treatment of the uncertainty from finite training samples.
StrengthIt works on real data and defines the whole production machinery — CI construction, validation, training-stat uncertainty. Everyone else builds on this.
LimitAs characterized by the CMS/refinable work (R4): two classifiers per knob (expensive for many systematics), a fixed (low) interpolation order, no non-factorizable knobs (assumes systematics act independently), kinematics unused in the interior |α|<1, and no use of joint-likelihood (truth) information.
R3 · Cranmer–Feickert–Heinrich–Held–Sandesara · SBI Blueprint, Feb 2026 · pyhf-gpsys

HistFactory v2 — Gaussian-Process morphing (with an error bar on the morphing)

IdeaTraditional morphing assumes (a) systematics factorize and (b) a rigid analytic interpolation shape (piecewise-linear/exponential). Replace that with a Gaussian Process: a flexible, data-driven interpolator that also reports its own uncertainty.
MechanismA Gaussian Process learns Δ(α) from the simulated anchor points (which may sit anywhere in knob-space, not just on the axes). For any new α it returns a Gaussian: a mean (the interpolated morphing) and a covariance (how unsure the interpolation is). That uncertainty becomes an extra constrained parameter γ that flows into the fit. Generalizes from binned to unbinned/SBI.
StrengthNon-parametric (no rigid ansatz), high-dimensional, no factorization assumption, and — uniquely — an honest uncertainty on the interpolation itself. In a low-statistics fit, that guards against the morphing silently being wrong.
LimitGP cost grows fast with the number of anchors; the kernel/prior is a modelling choice (injected smoothness bias); high-dim α still needs enough simulated anchors to pin down; pre-publication.
R4 · Barrué–Schöfbeck et al. · MLST 6 015007 (2025) + PRD 112 052006 (2025) · tool: GOLLUM

Refinable unbinned ML systematics — the most complete per-event recipe

IdeaSplit the per-event ratio into three learned parts — R(x|θ) (the physics POIs, e.g. κλ) × S(x|α) (the systematics) × g(x) (which process the event is) — and learn the systematics part with a flexible parametric model in the knobs.
MechanismWrite log[σ(α)/σ(0)] = αA·Δ̂A(x) with a basis A that can include linear, quadratic, and cross-terms between knobs. Fit the coefficient functions Δ̂A(x) from simulated basis points (binned) or learn them with a cross-entropy loss (unbinned). "Refinable" = start low-order/cheap, add terms to improve. Validate with Classifier Two-Sample Tests.
StrengthArbitrary order (captures non-linear systematic effects the ±1σ picture misses), handles non-factorizable knobs (cross-terms), uses kinematics across the whole α range, fewer estimators than ATLAS (log-linear), and exploits joint-likelihood info. Tested on your FAIR Universe benchmark: ~20% tighter 1σ on μ vs binned (1.00 ± 0.17 vs ± 0.21), with tighter calibration-systematic constraints.
LimitStill assumes a (flexible) functional form in α — no built-in interpolation-uncertainty like the GP. Needs simulated basis points. And the process-fraction calibration is make-or-break: dropping it biased μ by 0.3–0.4.
R5 · Harris et al. · PRD 111 032010 (2025) · 2403.07066

RS3L — robustness in the representation (the other philosophy)

IdeaDon't model the knob in the likelihood at all — build input features that are insensitive to it, so the systematic shrinks at the source.
MechanismSelf-supervised contrastive pretraining where the "same" example is the same hard event re-simulated with different shower/hadronization/detector settings. The encoder learns to give them the same embedding → invariant to those modelling variations.
StrengthAttacks the modelling systematics (shower/hadronization) that are hardest to parameterize as knobs; gives a reusable robust representation; complements a likelihood treatment.
LimitNeeds the re-simulation chain and constituent-level inputs; covers only the systematics it re-simulated (not luminosity/normalization); full invariance can discard signal info correlated with the knob (the pivot/info trade-off); and it still needs a likelihood/fit on top to get a CI.

2 · How they relate (the family tree)

Read R1 → R2 → {R3, R4} as one lineage, all "model the knob in the likelihood":

  • R1 says: parameterize on α, don't decorrelate. (the principle)
  • R2 realizes it per-event at full scale, with a fixed-order, factorized morphing. (the baseline)
  • R3 and R4 are two different upgrades of R2's morphing: R4 makes the parametric model far more expressive (arbitrary order, cross-terms, kinematics); R3 goes non-parametric and adds an uncertainty on the interpolation. They're complementary — you could even use a GP-style uncertainty on a refinable-style surrogate.

R5 is off this tree entirely — it changes the inputs, not the likelihood. It composes with any of R2–R4 rather than competing with them.

The headline trade-off inside the "model-it" camp: expressiveness vs. honesty. R4 buys you the most expressive morphing (it can fit complicated, interacting systematics); R3 buys you honesty (it tells you how much to trust the morphing). Neither is strictly better — and they're combinable.

4 · What fits HH→bb̄ττ / κλ

Your situation: a tiny signal, several backgrounds with their own systematics, many experimental knobs (b-tagging, τ-ID, JES, τES) and theory knobs (scale/PDF/parton-shower on the HH signal), and a measurement that's both statistics- and systematics-limited. κλ itself lives in the mHH shape, which is the POI part R(x|θ) — so the systematics are a separate factor you bolt on. A layered plan:

Foundation — adopt the machinery (R2). Follow the ATLAS NSBI template for the fit, the test statistic, confidence intervals, diagnostics, and the finite-training-stat uncertainty. This is the scaffolding you need regardless of which morphing you choose.
Core — use the refinable surrogate (R4) for the morphing. It is the closest fit: it's per-event, it cleanly separates κλ (R) from systematics (S) from process (g) — your κλ morphing slots straight into R — it handles the calibration knobs (JES/τES) and their interactions, and it's already validated on your FAIR Universe testbed with a real ~20% CI gain. This is the thing to reproduce first. Heed its warning: get the process-fraction calibration right, or you'll bias the result.
Honesty layer — add GP-style interpolation uncertainty (R3) for the dominant, sparsely-simulated knobs. HH has few signal events and few simulated systematic points → the morphing is genuinely uncertain. An error bar on the interpolation (not pretending it's exact) protects κλ from morphing-induced bias. Prototype with pyhf-gpsys.
Complement — RS3L-style robust inputs (R5) for shower/hadronization, later. These modelling systematics are hard to parameterize as knobs; a robust representation reduces them at the source. Bigger infrastructure lift (needs re-simulation), so treat it as a second-phase enhancement — and note this is exactly where your foundation-model / Sophon thread connects to the systematics story.
One-sentence answer. Build on ATLAS's NSBI machinery, use the refinable per-event surrogate (R4) as your systematics morphing because it's proven on your exact benchmark and handles your calibration knobs, add a GP-style uncertainty (R3) on the morphing because you're statistics-starved, and keep RS3L (R5) as a later, complementary lever for the parton-shower systematics — where your FM work already lives.

Check yourself

1. R3 and R4 are both "upgrades of ATLAS's morphing." What does each add that the other doesn't?

R4 adds expressiveness (arbitrary order, cross-terms, kinematics in a parametric model); R3 adds honesty (a non-parametric fit plus an uncertainty on the interpolation). They're combinable.

2. Why is RS3L "off the family tree"?

It doesn't build Δ(x|α) at all — it changes the input representation to be insensitive to the knob. It composes with R2–R4 rather than replacing them.

3. For κλ, which factor does the self-coupling live in, and which factor carries the systematics?

κλ lives in the POI factor R(x|θ) (the mHH shape morphing across coupling values); the systematics are the separate S(x|α) factor. They're modelled independently and multiplied.
