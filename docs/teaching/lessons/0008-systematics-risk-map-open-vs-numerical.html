The systematics risk map —
what's open, what's numerical

A literature-grounded triage of the six systematics challenges (plus two upsides) from the κλ-vs-off-shell note, §5. The one question that organises the risk: for each challenge, does the literature already give a recipe, or not?

Two very different kinds of risk hide in a to-do list, and conflating them wastes effort:

  • Conceptually open — no published method tackles this in a per-event/unbinned setting. You'd have to invent or non-trivially extend something. Research risk.
  • Numerical (quantification) — a method exists and is demonstrated; the only question is how bad it is in your setting, answerable by a measurement. Engineering risk.

The verdict below is sobering in a useful way: none of the six is cleanly numerical — bbττ consistently exceeds where any per-event method has been shown — but only one is fully open. The rest are hybrid: a method exists in an easier setting, so each "pilot" is really a method-selection gate, not just a number to measure.grounded: 2024–26 lit. sweep + maturity skeptic

The one fact that reframes everything: the largest published demonstrations of learned NP morphing are ~3 calibration NPs on simulated data (GOLLUM, PRD 112 052006) and ~50 NPs on real but clean 4ℓ data (ATLAS, 2412.01600). Nothing exercises the actual bbττ regime — ~40 shape-degenerate NPs deforming a reconstructed mHH across three channels with data-driven backgrounds.

2 · The one genuinely open item — C4 (κλ-coupled theory)

C1 and C4 sound alike ("κλ and a systematic both involve mHH"), but they are different kinds of problem living in different places in the model. The signal enters the likelihood as a factorised product P(x;κλ,α) = r(x;κλ) · ∏m wm(x;αm) — one POI-dependent ratio times one POI-independent weight per nuisance.

Why off-shell escaped it but κλ can't. Algebraically the two are identical — POI coefficients on fixed templates — so the difference isn't the POI structure; it's what the theory uncertainty acts on. THUHH sits on the signal, and triangle/box/interference carry different scale/PDF/mt bands; κλ sets their mixture, so the net theory deformation's size and mHH shape change with κλ — and the fractional band diverges at the κλ≈2.45 cross-section minimum where triangle and box cancel. Off-shell's leading theory NP is on the μ-independent gg→ZZ background, and σ(μ) is monotonic — no cancellation, no re-mixing.

Why "open," not "impossible" — the first thing to try: replace the single total-signal THUHH NP with per-component, POI-independent theory weights w△, wint, w□; the κλ coefficients then blend them and the κλ-dependence of the net THU emerges automatically, preserving factorisation. Missing pieces (hence open): the component-differential theory bands (the LHC HH WG tabulates only the total +4/−18%) and any NSBI closure of per-component theory morphing.

3 · The two that most threaten a tighter interval

NSBI's whole selling point is a narrower κλ CI — so the dangerous challenges are the ones that make a narrow interval mis-cover, not merely widen it.

C6 — the MC-stat estimator may be *wrong*, not just unvalidated. Shyamsundar (arXiv:2505.19156) gives a toy where the ATLAS double-bootstrap estimator of the finite-simulation uncertainty does not capture it. Since your ±1σ systematic-variation samples are the binding statistical constraint in bbττ, an estimator that under-covers there directly inflates apparent sensitivity. Settle which estimator covers before quoting a number.
C2 — the NP-scale jump may force a different method. ~40 shape-degenerate NPs × processes × ±1σ × ensembles ≈ thousands of NNs, never shown to stay calibrated with cross-terms. If the pilot shows cost or calibration blowing up, switch the primary morphing to a conditional surrogate (GOLLUM/R4) or GP morphing (R3) rather than the classifier-per-NP approach.

4 · The order to attack them

  1. Gates first (upstream of trusting any tighter interval): C4 — does a POI×NP ansatz even work, on a toy? and C6 — which MC-stat estimator covers?
  2. Then the C2 scaling gate — a method-selection decision (classifiers vs surrogate/GP).
  3. Then closures: C3 (data-driven density), C1 (per-event morphing accuracy), C5 (cross-term).
  4. In parallel, the field-first upsides: U1 (CARL/DCTR profiled NP), U2 (SF absorption + in-situ candle).

The shape of the whole answer: one open problem, two coverage threats, and a stack of method-selection gates dressed as quantification tasks. The trap would be to treat the six "hybrid" items as easy numbers — they're not, because bbττ sits past where any method has actually been demonstrated.
