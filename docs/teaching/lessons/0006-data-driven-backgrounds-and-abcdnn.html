Data-driven backgrounds in HH→bbττ —
and the ABCDnn bridge to NSBI

Your κλ measurement's hardest new problem isn't the signal model — it's that several bbττ backgrounds are estimated from data, and data-driven estimates don't naturally hand you the one thing NSBI needs: a per-event density. This lesson maps the bbττ background toolkit, then shows exactly where ABCDnn fits.

One question organises everything below: for each background, which of {shape, normalization} do I trust simulation for? The answer puts every process on a spectrum, and decides which ones cause NSBI trouble.

So the signal-region background in bbττ is a stack of different estimation methods — unlike the ATLAS off-shell H→ZZ→4ℓ measurement (Lesson 5), whose dominant background is a single irreducible qq̄→ZZ taken from MC and constrained in situ. That contrast is the new difficulty.

2 · Workhorse 1 — ABCD (for QCD multijet)

Pick two variables believed independent for the background — e.g. di-τ charge (OS/SS) and τ isolation (tight/anti-isolated). They carve four orthogonal regions; D is signal-like.

ND = NB · NC / NA the transfer factor NB/NA measured in the SS sidebands is applied to the OS axis (it can be a single number or a binned function of a kinematic variable)

One load-bearing assumption: the two variables factorize for the background (isolation behaviour independent of charge), and the background composition doesn't change across regions. If they correlate, the equation is biased.cites: CMS Open Data ABCD lesson · arXiv:2206.09401

What you get: a yield and a (typically 1-D, binned) shape. Enough for a histogram fit — not a per-event density. Hold that thought for §5.

(The exact two-variable choice and isolation working point are analysis-note-specific; don't read the OS/SS×iso labels above as the literal cuts of any one paper.)

3 · Workhorse 2 — the fake-factor method (for jet→τh)

A quark/gluon jet faking a hadronic τ is rare per jet, so measure the rate from data. Three regions, identical except the τ-ID requirement:

  • SR — tight τ-ID (where you want the prediction);
  • AR — loose-but-not-tight τ-ID, same kinematics/charge as SR (the events you reweight);
  • DR — a fake-enriched region (e.g. SS, or high mT(ℓ,MET) for W+jets) where you measure the rate.
FF = Ntight / (Nloose − Ngenuine) measured in the DR; genuine-τ contamination subtracted (else FF biases high) — then applied as a per-event weight to AR events

Assumption: the fake composition (quark/gluon/b/pileup mix) is stable from DR to SR, so the measured rate transfers. ATLAS's flavor-decomposed "universal" fake factor (arXiv:2502.04156) relaxes this. Typical FF systematics ~10–30%.cites: arXiv:2204.12957 · 1510.07488 · 2502.04156

What you get: per-event weights on AR events — a reweighted sample, not a function p(x) you can evaluate at an arbitrary point x. Hold that thought too.

4 · The ladder: ABCD → ExtendedABCD → ABCDnn

Same goal (a data-driven background in the signal region), increasing power to handle correlation and to deliver a real shape:

ABCD. Closed-form ND=NBNC/NA. Gives a yield + coarse binned shape. Assumes factorization — its single point of failure.
ExtendedABCD. Add sidebands to measure and correct the residual correlation instead of assuming it away. Still closed-form. In the CMS all-hadronic four-top analysis it improved closure from ~18–19% (standard ABCD, high jet multiplicity) to ~1–7%.illustrative — that analysis (S. An thesis, internal) Buys normalization accuracy; still only a low-dim shape.
ABCDnn. Replace the multiplicative factorization with a learned conditional density. A neural autoregressive flow (NAF — an invertible, monotonic map) is conditioned on the control variables (e.g. Nj, Nb) and trained on signal-depleted sidebands to learn pbg(x | control), then extrapolated to D. It does not assume independence — the flow learns arbitrary correlations, and it generates the full multivariate distribution, not just a yield. cites: arXiv:2008.03636 · NAF 1804.00779

The original ABCDnn (2008.03636) trains the flow by maximum likelihood; the CMS four-top deployment trains it via Maximum Mean Discrepancy and uses a yield/shape split — ExtendedABCD for the normalization, ABCDnn for the differential shape.

ABCD: assumes factorization → yield  •  ExtendedABCD: measures the breaking → better yield  •  ABCDnn: learns the conditional density → shape

5 · The crux — why this is an NSBI problem, and how ABCDnn bridges it

NSBI builds an unbinned likelihood from per-event density ratios r(x)=psig(x|κλ)/pref(x), and it needs a computable per-event probability for every component — including each background. But look at what the data-driven methods actually hand you:

MethodOutputIs it a per-event density p(x)?
ABCDyield + coarse histogramNo — a binned shape
fake-factorper-event weights on AR eventsNo — a reweighted sample, not an evaluable p(x)
τ-embeddinga sample of eventsNo — samples, no explicit p(x)

So NSBI cannot directly ingest the QCD / fake / DY estimates. This is the structural gap the off-shell measurement never faced (its background was one MC density).

ABCDnn is the natural bridge. ABCDnn already is a learned density pbg(x | control). To feed NSBI, train a classifier between flow-generated background events and the reference hypothesis — that converts the density into the per-event ratio r(x) NSBI consumes. Neural fake-factor estimation (arXiv:2511.06972) does the analogue for fakes: it recasts the fake factor as a neural density-ratio ploose(x)/ptight(x) in high-dimensional feature space — a continuous, unbinned, per-event FF(x) instead of a binned lookup, directly NSBI-compatible.
The honest gap (the real caveat). Every ABCDnn deployment to date targets a low-dimensional object — in the four-top analysis, just the BDT discriminant + HT, because the downstream fit is binned. NSBI for bbττ wants the full per-event density over the whole 10+-feature vector. That needs a high-dimensional flow (harder to train cleanly; non-negativity/monotonicity must hold everywhere), much more training data, and high-dimensional closure tests that are statistically punishing — plus every systematic propagated as a per-event morphing of r(x) (the learned-morphing burden from Lesson 4). No published bbττ NSBI consumes a full high-dimensional ABCDnn background density yet.

Pragmatic compromise to reach for first: use ABCDnn (or neural fake-factor) to model only the data-driven shape of the 1-D NSBI discriminant (the summed log-ratio score), treat QCD/fakes as a binned auxiliary term in the combined likelihood, and reserve full per-event morphing for the MC-modelled components (embedded-DY, tt̄, single-H, diboson) where it's tractable. You give up unbinned information on the fakes, but you get a buildable analysis. (A design proposal — not a published bbττ result.)
