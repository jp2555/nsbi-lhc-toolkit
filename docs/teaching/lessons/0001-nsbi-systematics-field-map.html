How does NSBI handle systematics?
A field map, 2021 → 2026

Tied to your mission: choosing a systematics treatment for the κλ HH→bb̄ττ NSBI fit, and seeing where RS3L-style robustness sits relative to modeling systematics in the likelihood.

Every method below is solving one problem. Get that, and the five papers become five answers to the same question — not five unrelated tricks.

The one problem

NSBI replaces binned templates with a per-event likelihood. Both 2026 blueprint talks write the same thing: the unbinned model is HistFactory, one factor per event instead of per bin:

P(x | η, χ)  ∝  ∏events e   [ Σsamples s   νs(η,ξ) · κs(η,ξ) · Δs(xe | α) · Ps(xe) ]   ·   ∏p f(ap|χp) η = POIs, ξ = unconstrained NPs, α = constrained nuisance parameters (the systematics). Last factor = constraint terms.

The green factor κ (rates: lumi, cross-section, POIs) has a known a-priori dependence — easy. The hard, irreducible piece is the systematic morphing Δs(x|α) = how the per-event density deforms as you dial a nuisance α (tau energy scale, JES, shower model…). It has no known functional form, the differential ratio is intractable, and α can live in 100–1000 dimensions. Every NSBI-systematics method is a different way to build a surrogate for Δ(x|α).

Two philosophies

Philosophy A

Model ν in the likelihood

Learn Δ(x|α) so the fit can profile over the nuisances. The systematic stays in the model; you pay with extra estimators and calibration. R1–R4 below.

Philosophy B

Make the representation robust

Change Ps(x) — the features the discriminant sees — to be insensitive to α, so the systematic shrinks instead of being modeled. R5 (RS3L).

The five works

R1 · Ghosh–Nachman–Whiteson · PRD 104 056026 (2021)

Uncertainty-aware learning — the seed idea

Don't make the network blind to nuisances (decorrelation/pivoting); make it aware: feed α as an input so the classifier learns the (x, α) correlation. Better sensitivity than forcing invariance.

Not yet a full likelihood/CI recipe — it establishes why later methods parameterize the ratio in α rather than decorrelating. 2105.08742

R2 · ATLAS · Rep. Prog. Phys. 88 (2025)

ATLAS NSBI — the first full-scale implementation

Per-event learned likelihood ratios with ~hundreds of systematics as nuisance parameters; profile-likelihood test statistic (qθ = tμ), confidence intervals, and explicit validation/diagnostics. Off-shell H→ZZ→4ℓ. Proves NSBI-with-systematics works on a real measurement.

Limitations (per the CMS blueprint, R4): the surrogate uses two classifiers per NP, a fixed order in α, ignores kinematics for |α|<1, assumes factorization (no non-factorizable NPs), and doesn't exploit joint likelihood ratios. 2412.01600

R3 · Cranmer–Feickert–Heinrich–Held–Sandesara · SBI Blueprint, Feb 2026

HistFactory v2 — Gaussian-Process morphing

Replace the analytic interpolation of Δ(α) with Gaussian-Process regression over the simulated anchor points. Non-parametric (no piecewise-linear/poly ansatz), high-dimensional (anchors need not be on orthogonal axes; no factorization), and — uniquely — it returns an uncertainty on the interpolation itself (the GP covariance), which becomes an extra constrained bin parameter γ. Works binned and unbinned/SBI; headed for ROOT/pyhf.

Cost: GP scaling, kernel/prior choice = injected inductive bias; pre-publication. pyhf-gpsys

R4 · Barrué–Schöfbeck et al. · MLST 6 015007 (2025) + PRD 112 052006 (2025)

Unbinned ML systematics — the "refinable" surrogate

Factor the per-event ratio into R(x|θ) (POIs) × S(x|α) (systematics) × g(x) (process fractions), then learn S with a parametric ansatz in the NPs: log[σ(α)/σ(0)] = αA Δ̂A(x). Arbitrary order (incl. cross-terms), handles non-factorizable NPs, uses kinematics to interpolate |α|<1, exploits joint likelihood ratios, and is "refinable" (start cheap, add orders). Validated with Classifier Two-Sample Tests.

Tested on your FAIR Universe benchmark (GOLLUM): ~20% tighter 1σ on μ vs binned (μ = 1.00 ± 0.17 unbinned vs ± 0.21 binned), with tighter calibration-systematic constraints. Caveat: process-fraction calibration is critical — dropping it biased μ by 0.3–0.4.

R5 · Harris et al. · PRD 111 032010 (2025)

RS3L — robustness in the representation (Philosophy B)

Self-supervised contrastive pretraining where the positive pairs are the same hard event re-simulated with varied shower/hadronization/detector. The embedding becomes insensitive to exactly those modeling systematics → any downstream classifier on it moves less under them.

Limits: needs the re-simulation chain and constituent-level inputs; covers modeling systematics it was re-simulated over (not e.g. luminosity); and full invariance can discard signal information correlated with the nuisance — so it's partial, the pivot/info trade-off. 2403.07066

R1 (uncertainty-aware) is the conceptual basis for parameterizing the ratio in α at all, so it sits "above" the table.

What this means for your κλ analysis. Your fit is systematics- and stat-limited, so the morphing surrogate Δ(x|α) is where the CI is won or lost. R4 is the closest fit — same FAIR Universe benchmark, per-event, arbitrary-order, handles the calibration nuisances you care about — and it's the natural target to reproduce on your testbed. R3 (GP) is attractive precisely because it gives an uncertainty on the interpolation, which is the honest way to avoid morphing-induced bias in a low-stat regime. R5 (RS3L) is the orthogonal lever you already scoped: it doesn't model α, it shrinks it — best treated as a complement (a robust input representation), measured by your robustness diagnostic, not a replacement for the likelihood treatment.

Check yourself (retrieval > re-reading)

1. In one sentence, what is the single quantity every NSBI-systematics method approximates?

The systematic morphing Δs(x|α): how the per-event probability density of sample s deforms as a function of the nuisance parameters α — the factor with no known a-priori functional form.

2. What distinguishes RS3L's philosophy from the ATLAS / HistFactory-v2 / refinable approaches?

RS3L changes the representation Ps(x) to be insensitive to α (shrinks the systematic); the others model α inside the likelihood and profile over it. Robust-representation vs model-in-likelihood.

3. Name the one capability the GP approach (R3) has that the others do not.

An uncertainty estimate on the interpolation itself (the GP posterior covariance), which can be propagated as an extra constrained parameter — robustness against the morphing being wrong.
